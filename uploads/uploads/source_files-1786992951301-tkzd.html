The Distressed Asset Opportunity

India auctions tens of thousands of crores of seized property every year through portals that publish notices but hide outcomes. A market-intelligence layer — not another listings board — is the gap. This paper sizes it, benchmarks the incumbents, and models a five-city pilot for bankauctioniq.com.

01 · What we concluded

Five findings that shape the product

The listings problem is solved. The decision problem is not.

BAANKNET, bankeauctions.com, eauctionsindia.com and a long tail of aggregators already publish nearly every SARFAESI notice. None of them answer the buyer's actual questions: what is this asset worth, what did comparable lots actually fetch, will I get physical possession, and how many bidders will I face? That asymmetry is the business.

Falling NPAs are not falling supply.

Gross NPAs are at multi-decadal lows, yet SARFAESI referrals held flat at ~2.16 lakh cases in FY24 and FY25 and recovery value rose. A smaller, cleaner loan book with far more aggressive enforcement produces a stable — not shrinking — flow of auction lots.

Nobody agrees how many lots exist in Chennai.

Four aggregators report 466, 985, 1,562 and 3,476 Chennai listings simultaneously. Duplicate notices, stale lots and re-auctions are counted as new. Being the portal whose count is correct and dated is a defensible wedge, and the raw material for every intelligence product downstream.

The international template is a subscription data business.

Auction.com monetises transaction volume; the UK's EIG monetises knowledge — every lot past and future, price history, alerts — and has done so profitably for decades on a market of 38,000 lots a year. India lists more than twice that and has no equivalent.

Revenue comes from the transaction, not the subscription.

At a ₹299–499 retail price point, subscriptions cover roughly a third of our modelled Year-5 revenue. Concierge fees, loan-arrangement commissions and success fees on closed deals carry the rest — because one ₹45 lakh purchase is worth ~150 subscription-months.

02 · Market size, FY23 → FY26

Three years of enforcement, digitised

The last three years contain the single most important structural change in this market: the migration of public sector bank auctions onto one portal. BAANKNET (formerly eBKray) became operational across most PSBs on 1 July 2024 and was formally launched in January 2025; all 12 public sector banks and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India now list on it.

The effect on volume was immediate. Average annual successful bid value rose to ₹15,731 crore for the twelve months to June 2025, against ₹5,267 crore on the predecessor portal in the comparable prior period — roughly a threefold jump. The auction success rate improved from 9% to 14.7%. Photographs and video of lots, previously absent, are credited by bank executives with much of that gain.

Underneath the platform story, enforcement volume is remarkably stable. SARFAESI referrals were 2,16,571 cases (₹1.19 lakh crore) in FY24 and 2,15,709 cases (₹1.03 lakh crore) in FY25, with recoveries rising from ₹30,416 crore to ₹32,466 crore and the recovery rate improving from 25.4% to 31.5%. Asset quality, meanwhile, kept improving: gross NPAs of scheduled commercial banks fell to 2.3% in March 2025 and 2.15% in September 2025.

Read this carefully

A 14.7% success rate means the market's real problem is unsold inventory, not scarce inventory.

For banks, every failed auction is a re-notification, a fresh valuation and another quarter of carrying cost. For buyers, the same lot reappearing at a lower reserve is the single most profitable pattern in the market — and nobody publishes it. Both sides pay for the same dataset.

03 · The next five years

FY27 → FY31: our projection, and where it could be wrong

No published forecast exists for Indian bank-auction volumes. What follows is our own model, built from three observable drivers, and it should be treated as an inference rather than a citation: (i) SARFAESI referral volume, flat for two years; (ii) bank credit growing 11–12% a year, which mechanically expands the future default base even at constant NPA ratios; (iii) auction success rate improving from 14.7% as photography, video and unified listing mature.

We model PSB auction value compounding at 12% a year off the ₹15,731 crore FY25 base, and assume the non-PSB market — private banks, NBFCs, housing finance companies, ARCs, DRT and IBC liquidation sales, all of which currently sit outside a single portal — is approximately equal in size again. That gives an all-India transacted market of roughly ₹31,000 crore in FY26 rising to about ₹55,000 crore by FY31.

Upside case

Unsecured retail and microfinance stress — which the RBI and S&P have both flagged — converts into secured enforcement with a two-to-three-year lag. Success rates climbing toward 25% would lift transacted value faster than volume.

Downside case

Auction listing changes borrower behaviour: UCO Bank's chief executive reports defaulters approaching the bank for one-time settlement once listed. Widespread pre-auction settlement shrinks completed-sale volume even as notice volume holds.

Platform risk

BAANKNET is state-backed, free, and already holds the PSB monopoly. If it publishes outcome data itself, our headline moat narrows to non-PSB coverage, analytics and service. Build for that world, not against it.

04 · The competitive set

Ten Indian portals, and the one thing each of them sells

Ranked by a blend of institutional weight and audience. Traffic figures are Similarweb estimates for October 2024 — the most recent public numbers we could verify — and should be read as order-of-magnitude, not precise. Where a site publishes its own counters we say so.

01

BAANKNET

baanknet.com · PSB Alliance Pvt Ltd

USP: the official monopoly. All 12 public sector banks plus IBBI list here; IBBI has mandated it for liquidation asset sales. Bank-verified titles, automated KYC, integrated payment gateway, photos and video.

Self-reported: 87,604 properties listed, 2,72,362 auctions conducted, ₹20,873 cr auctioned, 3,044 upcoming. Free to buyers.

02

bankeauctions.com

C1 India Pvt Ltd

USP: the incumbent transaction engine. Positions itself as India's most trusted portal for foreclosed movable and immovable assets, having run auctions for almost every leading public and private bank.

Bank-branded landing pages (SBI, PNB and others), forward auctions and DRT sales. Revenue from banks, not buyers.

03

eauctionsindia.com

~660K visits/month (Oct 2024)

USP: the biggest consumer audience in the category. Aggregates notices across SARFAESI, NPA, DRT and liquidation, sliced by city, bank and asset class — and it wins on SEO, not on tooling.

Claims 574 banks and 639 cities. Free, ad-monetised, including a gold-auction vertical. The traffic leader we must displace.

04

Auction Tiger

auctiontiger.in · e-Procurement Technologies

USP: auction infrastructure at scale. Claims over 1 lakh auctions with ~45,000 fresh listings, spanning central and state government, bank and global sales — a procurement platform that also does NPAs.

Enterprise-first UX. Strong for institutional sellers, hostile to a first-time retail buyer.

05

IBAPI

ibapi.in · ~88K visits/month (Oct 2024)

USP: the original government-backed PSB aggregator, now largely superseded by BAANKNET. Historically the reference point for "official" listings.

Instructive as a cautionary tale: official status without product quality did not produce audience.

06

foreclosureindia.com

Independent aggregator

USP: multi-seller breadth — banks, liquidators, DRTs and financial institutions in one searchable database — plus daily email alerts on saved filters and a gold vertical.

Closest in intent to what we are building; still notice-level data with no outcomes.

07

bankauctiondeals.com

Independent aggregator

USP: the deal-hunter framing — "20–60% below market" — plus a free WhatsApp channel for state-level alerts and per-lot source-notice traceability across 200+ lenders.

Claims 24,401+ live SARFAESI listings nationally. The WhatsApp habit is the one growth mechanic worth copying outright.

08

findauction.in

Independent aggregator

USP: locality-level search with reserve price, EMD and inspection dates surfaced up front, and asset-class counts published per city — the most transparent taxonomy in the set.

Reports 3,476 Chennai lots — seven times ForeclosureIndia's 466 for the same city. Nobody reconciles.

09

bankauctions.in / 4Closure

Independent aggregator, since 2019

USP: an explicit "free, fair and transparent" positioning against paywalled and lead-selling competitors, organised around participating banks.

Proof that "free and honest" alone does not fund a data business.

10

samil.in + city specialists

~191K visits/month (Oct 2024)

USP: movables done properly. Shriram Automall runs real bidding events for repossessed cars, bikes, trucks, tractors and construction equipment — the asset classes property portals treat as an afterthought. Alongside it, hyperlocal players like chennaibankauction.com aggregate 170+ organisations for one city.

The vehicle and machinery segment has a separate audience, cadence and trust model. Our multi-asset scope must respect that.

The pattern across all ten

Every incumbent monetises the notice — as traffic, as ad inventory, or as a fee from the bank. Not one monetises the outcome. No Indian portal will tell you what the last comparable lot actually sold for.

05 · Feature benchmark

India versus the three best international platforms

We benchmarked against Auction.com (largest US marketplace for distressed, bank-owned and foreclosed property), Hubzu and Xome (Altisource and Mr. Cooper-lineage REO auction platforms), and the UK's Essential Information Group — the only one of the four that is a pure data subscription, and the closest analogue to what we intend to build.

Auction.com

7.5 million registered users. 12,000+ auctions a year. 5,33,000 properties sold worth $66 billion, with ~13,000 live at a time.

Model: scale marketplace. Buyer's premium and technology fees on transactions.

Hubzu / Xome

Auto-bid and backup offers; per-listing property features, sales history and neighbourhood data; mobile apps with outbid and auction-ending push alerts; Xome's "First Look" exclusivity window.

Model: ~5% buyer's premium; Hubzu also charges sellers a $995 program fee, refunded on sale.

EIG (UK)

Every lot coming to auction and every lot offered in the past — 850,000+ properties, 38,000 new lots a year worth over £4.7 billion, 530+ auctioneers covered, and Auction Insights built on 600,000+ sold lots.

Model: paid membership. Alerts, price history, comparables, due diligence. The proof that outcome data sells.

07 · The moat

Ten market-intelligence products nobody in India sells today

Each of these is buildable from public material — sale notices, auction results, registration and guideline values, court listings, satellite and street imagery, our own bidder telemetry — and each answers a question a buyer currently cannot answer at any price. Ordered by how much we expect people to pay.

01 · Flagship

True Clearing Price index

Hammer price against reserve for every completed auction, by micro-market and asset class, with sold/unsold outcome. This is EIG's 600,000-lot dataset, built for India. It is the single most valuable number in the market and no Indian portal publishes it.

02 · Flagship

Re-auction Risk & Discount Ladder

With a 14.7% success rate, most lots come back. We track every relisting of the same asset and show how far each bank has historically cut its reserve on the second, third and fourth attempt — turning "should I bid now or wait?" into an evidenced decision.

03

Possession Reality Score

Symbolic versus physical possession, occupancy, pending stay orders, society and utility arrears, tenancy — scored per lot, with historical time-to-possession by district and lender. The risk buyers most fear and understand least.

04 · You asked for this

Auction heat maps

Locality-level maps layering live lot density, average discount to guideline value, clearing-rate and bidder competition — filtered to each user's preferred areas and price band. The most shareable artefact we can build, and a strong free-tier hook with the depth paywalled.

05

Bidder Competition Forecast

Expected number of qualified bidders and the likely winning band for a given lot, modelled from our own watchlist and EMD telemetry plus historical comparables. Nobody else has the demand-side data to compute this.

06

Lender Behaviour Profiles

How each bank actually behaves: reserve aggressiveness versus market, willingness to accept the sole bid, cancellation frequency, sale-certificate turnaround, litigation rate. Useful to buyers; extremely saleable back to the banks themselves as a benchmark.

07

90-Day Supply Pipeline

Section 13(2) demand notices and possession notices published before sale notices, mapped to locality — a forward view of what is likely to come to auction next quarter. The professional investor's reason to renew.

08

Exit & Yield Model

Per-lot resale liquidity, expected days-on-market, achievable rent and net yield after stamp duty, arrears and refurbishment — so the buy decision includes the exit, not just the entry discount.

09

Movables intelligence

For cars, bikes, commercial trucks and plant: reserve against fair market value by make, model, year and hours run; fleet-age and idle-time signals; district-level resale spreads. A neglected, high-frequency, high-repeat segment.

10 · Distribution, not data

WhatsApp intelligence briefs

A daily or weekly brief on the user's chosen localities and asset classes, delivered where Indian buyers already are, plus a curated mailing list. One competitor runs a WhatsApp alerts channel; none pairs it with genuine analysis. This is our cheapest retention lever.

The compounding argument

Items 01, 02, 05 and 07 can only be built by someone who has been watching every auction, every day, for years. They cannot be bought, and a competitor starting in 2029 cannot backfill them.

Two caveats we should state plainly to any investor. First, auction results are not uniformly published in India — a meaningful share of our clearing-price dataset will have to be assembled from sale certificates, registration records, bank confirmations and our own concierge closures, which is operationally expensive. Second, publishing borrower-linked information demands real care on data protection under the DPDP Act; the product should surface asset facts, never borrower profiles.

08 · Product

Five features that win subscribers, and the concierge that monetises them

Top 5 features — free hook, paid depth

1. One verified feed with a real countdown

Every lot across banks, NBFCs, ARCs, DRT and liquidation — deduplicated, dated, with EMD deadline, inspection window and source notice attached. Free: search and browse. Paid: unlimited saved searches, instant WhatsApp and push alerts, early access window.

Why it wins: aggregators currently disagree with each other by 7× on a single city.

2. Fair-Value Verdict on every lot

Reserve price against guideline value, portal asking prices and actual clearing prices for comparable lots, expressed as one honest verdict: underpriced, fair, or optimistic. Free: the verdict badge. Paid: the comparables, the clearing band, the workings.

Why it wins: "20–60% below market" is an unverified claim on every competitor site.

3. Risk & Possession report

A traffic-light on possession type, litigation, arrears and title clarity, with plain-language explanation of what each flag means for a buyer. Free: the light. Paid: the full report and the encumbrance pull.

Why it wins: it addresses the exact fear that keeps first-time buyers out of auctions entirely.

4. Heat maps and the Discount Index

Where the deals actually are this month, by locality and asset class, with discount, clearing-rate and competition layers. Free: current lot density. Paid: discount, clearing and competition layers plus twelve months of history.

Why it wins: it is the most shareable object in the product and drives organic acquisition.

5. Bid-to-possession workflow

EMD calculator and deadline tracker, document checklist, pre-approved auction finance from partner lenders, then concierge handoff and a possession tracker after the hammer falls. Free: calculator and checklist. Paid: the assisted path.

Why it wins: it converts intent into the revenue lines that actually pay for the company.

Concierge, productised

Tier 1 · Diligence · ₹25,000

Title and encumbrance verification, a physical site visit with dated photos and video, occupancy check, arrears and litigation search, written go/no-go with a recommended maximum bid.

Tier 2 · Represented bid · ₹45,000

Everything in Tier 1, plus EMD and demand-draft handling, KYC and portal registration, a bidding strategy with walk-away price, and a named specialist on the phone through the auction window.

Tier 3 · Keys in hand · ₹65,000 + 0.75%

End to end: loan tie-up, sale certificate follow-through, registration and mutation, physical possession including eviction support, then optional resale or rent-out. Success fee only on a completed purchase.

Concierge is also our data acquisition engine. Every engagement produces a verified valuation, a confirmed possession outcome and — critically — a hammer price. The service pays for the dataset that makes the subscription worth buying.

Operational caution: concierge is people-intensive and does not scale like software. Cap it deliberately, price it to a 55–60% gross margin, and staff it city by city rather than promising national coverage in Year 1.

09 · Admin module

What the back office must do on day one

The admin module is not an afterthought — it is where the product's only real asset, data quality, is manufactured. Specified here at the level a design and engineering team can build from.

Ingestion & notice pipeline

Scheduled crawls per source, PDF and newspaper-notice OCR, field extraction into a review queue, duplicate and relisting detection against existing lots, source-notice attachment for traceability, ingest health dashboard with per-source failure alerts.

Master data

Lender, branch and authorised-officer registers; ARC, liquidator and DRT registers; asset taxonomy across immovable and movable classes; locality and pincode geo-master with map polygons; guideline and circle-value tables by state; vehicle make-model-variant master.

Lot editor & verification

Single-lot workspace: correct extracted fields, tag possession type, upload site photos and video, attach encumbrance certificates, set risk flags, assign a verification level and verifier, then publish with a full change history.

Outcome capture

The most important screen in the system: record sold or unsold, hammer price, bidder count, cancellation reason and re-auction linkage, with a provenance field for how the outcome was confirmed. This is what the entire intelligence layer is built on.

Auction calendar & alerts

Calendar across all sources; EMD and inspection deadline management; alert template library for WhatsApp, email and push; segment builder; send scheduling with throttles; deliverability and open reporting.

Subscriptions & entitlements

Plan and price-book management, coupons and trials, per-feature entitlement flags, payment and refund ledger, GST invoicing, dunning, churn and cohort reporting.

Concierge case management

Case queue with SLA timers, specialist assignment by city, document vault, site-visit scheduling, deliverable templates, fee and success-fee tracking, and closure capture that feeds the outcome database.

Partner & lead desk

Onboarding for lenders, advocates, valuers and surveyors; commission schedules; lead routing rules; conversion and payout reconciliation per partner.

Content, SEO & heat-map config

City and locality landing-page templates, editorial and FAQ management, redirect rules, plus configuration of heat-map layers, bands and refresh cadence without a deploy.

Access, audit & compliance

Role-based access for admin, data operations, verifier, concierge, finance and partner roles; immutable audit log on every published field; DPDP consent and data-retention controls; borrower-data redaction rules enforced at publish time.

Analytics & data quality

Coverage by city and lender against expected notice volume, freshness and staleness monitors, duplicate rate, outcome-capture rate, funnel and revenue dashboards, and an API key manager for institutional clients.

10 · Honest risks

What would change our mind

Outcome data may be harder to get than modelled

Our entire moat assumes we can reliably observe hammer prices. If banks and portals do not disclose them and registration data lags by months, the flagship product degrades into an estimate. Test this in Chennai within 90 days before committing capital to five cities.

A free state platform is a hard competitor

BAANKNET has the PSB monopoly, official trust and no price. We must be complementary — non-PSB coverage, analytics, service — rather than a paid substitute for something free.

Willingness to pay is unproven at this price point

No Indian competitor charges retail buyers for auction data, so our 3% conversion assumption has no local benchmark. EIG proves the model in the UK on a professional buyer base, not a retail one.

Liability on advice

A "fair value verdict" and a "risk score" invite claims when a deal sours. Product copy, disclaimers, professional indemnity cover and a clear line between information and advice all need legal review before launch.

Concierge margin drag

Half our modelled revenue is a services business wearing a platform's valuation multiple. Investors will discount it. Either automate the diligence steps aggressively or present concierge honestly as a margin-diluting acquisition channel.

Data-source fragility

Ingestion depends on portals and newspaper notices whose formats change without warning and whose terms of use may restrict scraping. Direct lender agreements are the durable answer and should start in Year 1.
